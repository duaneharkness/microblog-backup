Why micro.blog?

Nov 9, 2026

I wanted a place to talk about my interests and share my opinions without the noise, ads, and intrusive data collection of mainstream social media. So far, micro.blog looks like the right fit.

Years ago I had a blog on Blogspot, but that service has gone fallow, so I needed somewhere new to write.

Heavyweight options like WordPress are great for professional blogs, but they’re too much effort for what I want to do. Many microblogging services have the opposite problem. They make short, low-effort posts easy, but they don’t allow for the occasional longer, more thoughtful post. I want room for both.

I’m also a fan of open systems, so I’d rather be on something open and decentralized. That means use of open standards like RSS and JSON Feed, full content and domain ownership, and implementation of the W3C ActivityPub standard. After a quick survey, micro.blog seemed like a good fit.

There’s one obvious downside. The user base is pretty small compared to mainstream platforms like Substack or Medium. That’s fine with me, because I’m not going for maximum exposure. If my friends, family, and search engines can find me, that’s enough.

Setting it up was fairly straightforward, though I did have to ask my AI assistant for help with several settings. I also decided to write and publish my drafts from Obsidian, which took a bit longer to set up since I didn’t have it installed.

One thing confused me for a while: how to set up a test blog, so I could publish a draft and see how it looks without changing my main blog. It turns out micro.blog sets one up for you in advance. It’s at your-blog-name-preview.micro.blog.

So what will I actually write about? Whatever I’m into at the moment. That includes listening to and making music, commenting on current events, birding, learning Spanish, travel, and working on software projects for fun. Some of it will be quick posts, and some will be longer pieces like this one.
